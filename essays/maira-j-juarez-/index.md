## Subheader 1 Midterm: The Journey Historical context and impacts (4-8 words)

For this midterm assignment, I will discuss my mom and our family and how migration has affected family relationships, tradition, and generations. Migration not only changes where a family lives but also how it shapes tradition, cultural identity, and family connection, and how these are passed from one generation to the next. My family is from Oaxaca, Mexico, where they speak their Native Language aside from Spanish, and my grandparents spoke the native language fluently and continued to speak it as they immigrated from Oaxaca, Mexico, to Santa Monica, California, and that’s where my mom's story starts. My mom started growing up in Santa Monica, California, until they moved to Los Angeles, California, where they had a more stable home. That's where she mainly lived in Los Angeles until she was 22 years old. Even though she grew up in Los Angeles, California, she was also fluent in our Native Language and Spanish because her first language was English. 
	My mom grew up speaking three separate languages, and continued even though she lived in California; she found her Hispanic community and continued to follow the steps with my grandparents with the traditions, food, and much more. In Oaxaca, Mexico, we have a really native dish that is called Mole, and it's so delicious, but when you talk about mole, it is a major cooking dish. Even though my mom grew up in California and she knows how to cook Mole from scratch, and this is the recipe from my grandmother, for my family these are huge accomplishments because there is only about 1% of my family members who know how to cook Mole and speak three languages; it's amazing.
	Once she started dating my dad, who is also Mexican from Oaxaca, he mentioned to her that he wanted to marry her and for them to start a family and live in Santa Fe, New Mexico. Mom mentioned that she refused that didn't want to move because home for her was California. Once they got engaged and married, my dad and I finally moved to New Mexico. My mom even mentioned that she never unpacked until she knew they weren't going back to California. Through this process, my parents were presenting a new place for our family in New Mexico because no one had been to or heard of New Mexico. So this affected our relationship for better growth, expanding our family and tradition, and events continued regardless of the location we remained. 
	In my opinion, these relate to the reading we have read, such as “Talk to Me, Milagros,” and how the class concept shows the chain of migration and the family connection with this and the expansion of the family, and this influenced the family to see a new place.  And how this relates to the connection between generations and how my mother continued to pass down the food and traditions to future generations. The next reading that relates to my family story is “trying to live without a place in the world,” and the reason why is because of how they mentioned the formation of youth identities and how that involves identities while living in the United States. The third reading for this is “Children of Loneliness,” and the reason why is because of the family traditions and the different generations may experience culture and belonging differently. These three readings relate to my family story of family migration, family relationships, and cultural identity. 
	In conclusion, my mother's migration story taught me that moving to a place is so significant, but it is so much more than changing where a family lives. This is an important step in shaping family relationships, traditions, and cultural identity across generations. Her life changed when she moved from California to New Mexico, and she continued to carry the traditions to shape our family identity. Her story is a reminder for me that migration does not mean leaving your culture behind; instead, you find a way to preserve it while building a life somewhere new.
